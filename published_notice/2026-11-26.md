# Parliament Pulse: Privacy notice

Version 2026-11-26, in force from 26 November 2026.

## About this notice

Parliament Pulse (parliamentpulse.co.uk) is an independent political research and data journalism project. It publishes data about UK politics and Parliament, lets readers explore and chart it, and lets people with an account comment and share what they make.

It is run by Samuel Kinshuck, an individual in the United Kingdom, who is the controller of the personal data described here: the person who decides how it is used and is responsible for it.

To ask anything about your personal data, or to use any of the rights below, use the [Your data page](/privacy/request). You do not need an account.

This notice covers two groups of people. Sections 1 to 3 are about people who **use** the site. Section 4 is about people who **appear in the data**: parliamentarians, election candidates, political donors and others named in public political records. The remaining sections apply to everyone.

## 1. If you use the site

You can read the site without an account and without telling us who you are.

**If you create an account** we keep your email address, a scrambled (hashed) form of your password that cannot be turned back into the password, your screen name and every screen name you have had, when you signed up and last logged in, which version of the Terms you accepted, and your choices about emails. We use these to run your account and to write to you about it. The legal basis is our contract with you (the [Terms](/terms)).

**The sign-up form asks your age band.** We count the answers by month to understand who comes to the site, as the online safety law requires. The answer is not stored with your account or linked to you in any way.

**What you post is public.** Comments, shared charts, scores and new columns appear under your screen name, on the page where you posted them and on your profile, to everyone or to logged-in members as you choose.

**Your likes and dislikes are public too.** When you like or dislike a comment, a chart, a parliamentarian, a candidate, a donor or a constituency, anyone can see that your account did so: in the list of who liked or disliked it, and on your profile. Liking or disliking a politician or a party donor can reveal your political opinions. Do not use likes and dislikes for anything you would not say in public under your screen name. You can withdraw one at any time by clicking it again.

**Comments about politics reveal political opinions**, which the law treats as a special category of personal data. We publish them because you chose to post them publicly (UK GDPR Article 9(2)(e)).

**Emails.** We send emails about your account (confirming your address, resetting a password, a decision to remove something of yours or restrict your account). If you agree, we also email you when people respond to what you posted; you can turn those off under **Email notifications** in the account menu.

**Blocking.** If you block another account we keep a record of that so the block works. Only you can see whom you have blocked, and nobody is told they have been blocked.

**How the site is used.** When a saved score, new column or shared chart is opened, we record which one, when, and the account that opened it, or the internet address if nobody was logged in. We use this to decide what to keep ready in memory, to limit heavy use and to show how often something has been used. The legal basis is our legitimate interest in running the site efficiently. We do not use advertising or tracking services, and we do not sell or share this information.

## 2. Keeping the site safe and lawful

The site is a regulated service under the Online Safety Act 2023. We have to keep illegal content off it and deal with reports, appeals and complaints. Doing that uses personal data in these ways. Unless we say otherwise, the legal basis is our legitimate interest in keeping the site safe and lawful, together with our legal obligations under that Act. A person, not a tool, makes every decision to remove content or restrict an account. Section 8 of the [Terms](/terms) describes each tool in full.

- **A text filter and a word list** on our own server check what you post. A word-list match is recorded against the comment and only moves it up the moderator's list.
- **OpenAI's moderation service.** We may send each comment to OpenAI (United States) together with what it needs to be read in context: what the comment is on (the name of the parliamentarian, candidate, constituency, payer or country whose page it is on, which for a payer may be a private person, with the party of a parliamentarian or candidate; or the title, description and labels of the chart, score or new column it is under), the comments it replies to and up to two other replies posted just before it in the same conversation. We never send a screen name, an account or an email address. We keep whether the service flagged the comment, in which categories and with what scores. While a flag is waiting for a moderator, the comment is kept from rising in the orders that depend on likes, dislikes and replies (it is placed there as if it had no likes, dislikes or replies), unless the account that posted it has a good record. The page is not told which comments are held.
- **A record of how each account's comments have fared** (left up, flagged, reported, found close to the line, or removed), worked out when needed and seen only by the moderator. Its only effect is the one just described.
- **An AI-assisted review.** Public comments, replies, shared charts and the publicly visible discussion around them, including public screen names and account IDs, may be reviewed using AI-assisted tools, including ChatGPT provided by OpenAI, to mark items a person should look at. It is given only what readers of the site can read, which includes charts and comments shared with logged-in members only, and nothing a moderator removed (nor the comments under a chart, score or new column that has been removed, deleted or made private). It is not told which comments were reported or flagged, by a reader, an automated check or an earlier review. So that the comments most in need of a look are read first, the site may give it reported or flagged comments before the others, without saying which they are. It is never given email addresses, who reported anything, how many reports a comment has, what a report said or what an author deleted. We keep the assistant's assessment of each item, and a record of exactly which comments it was given and when. This processing may take place outside the United Kingdom.
- **Reports, appeals and complaints.** We keep what you send, your account if you were logged in, the email address if you chose to leave one, and for a short time the internet address it came from (to stop the forms being flooded). The person who posted something is never told who reported it.
- **The moderator's decisions.** We keep a log of what was decided about which item or account and why, with a copy of the item as it was. A moderator may also mark a report as made in bad faith.
- **What you delete** is kept out of public view for a time before it is erased, because a report may arrive afterwards and because the law requires child sexual abuse material to be reported to the National Crime Agency.
- **Stopping a banned person coming back.** We keep a scrambled form of the internet addresses each account logs in and posts from, made with a secret key that is not kept with it, so that the stored form cannot be turned back into the address. When we remove an account, or suspend one for a serious breach such as child sexual abuse or terrorism, or a moderator decides in another serious case that it is needed, we keep a hashed form of its email address and bar new accounts from that address and, for a time, from those internet addresses. For child sexual abuse and terrorism the legal basis is the prevention of crime (UK GDPR Article 6(1)(ea)); otherwise our legitimate interest in enforcing the rules.

None of this is a decision made solely by automated means that has a legal or similarly significant effect on you. If you think a tool has treated you wrongly, tell us from the [Report or appeal page](/report).

## 3. Cookies and storage in your browser

We use no advertising or tracking cookies, and there is no cookie banner because nothing here needs your consent. What the site keeps in your browser is:

- **A login cookie**, set when you log in, so that you stay logged in (for up to 30 days). It cannot be read by scripts in the page.
- **Small notes that you have seen something**, so that an introduction or a warning is not shown to you again.
- **Your display choices**, such as the order you like comments in and how many rows a list shows.
- **A note that this browser has already answered the sign-up form's age question**, so that one person's answer is counted once.

These stay on your device and are not sent anywhere except, for the login cookie, back to this site.

## 4. If you appear in the data

Parliament Pulse publishes information about people in public political life: members of the House of Commons and the House of Lords, past and present; candidates at parliamentary elections; people and organisations who donate to parties and politicians; and people named in the Register of Members' Financial Interests, such as payers, employers and family members a parliamentarian employs.

**Where it comes from.** We did not collect it from you. It comes from public sources: the UK Parliament's own data services and Hansard, the Register of Members' Financial Interests, the Electoral Commission's register of donations, published election results, Companies House, Wikidata and Wikipedia. Every value on the site can be traced to its source: click a label and choose where the value came from.

**What it includes.** Names; parliamentary and electoral careers; parties; votes; declared interests and donations; dates and places of birth; education, occupation and family connections to other parliamentarians, where public sources give them; portraits from public sources; and, for parliamentarians, scores our own statistical model derives from their recorded votes.

**Guesses.** Some values are not facts taken from a source but guesses we made, and the site marks them as such wherever they appear:

- For **parliamentarians**, religion, denomination and sexuality are, in many cases, guesses made by an AI language model (provided by OpenAI) that was given the person's Wikipedia biography. They are labelled "AI guessed", with a warning that they may be wrong, and religion and denomination carry the model's own rating of its confidence, which is not a measured accuracy. For sexuality the model had to choose one of two answers, heterosexual or not: it had no "unknown" answer, and it was told to assume heterosexual whenever it could not tell and to give the other answer where a person was rumoured not to be heterosexual. The site shows the two answers as "Heterosexual" and "Likely not heterosexual", so "Heterosexual" here includes everyone about whom the biography said nothing.
- For **election candidates, donors and other payers** who are not parliamentarians, gender and religious background are guessed from the name alone by fixed rules, and are labelled as a guess from the name with the part of the name that produced them. Such a guess says what a name suggests. It may reflect a naming tradition or a family background and not the person's beliefs, and most people have no guess at all. A candidate, donor or payer who is also a parliamentarian is shown with the value from their parliamentary record instead, which for religion is the AI model's guess.

A guess is not a statement by us that the person holds that belief or identity.

**Why we do this, and the legal basis.** The purpose is independent research and data journalism about British political institutions: who is elected and who stands, who funds politics, how parliamentarians vote, and how different groups of people are represented in political life.

A fundamental principle of Parliament Pulse is that users should be able to examine the evidence behind data, analysis and visualisations shown on the site. Users can check every figure against the individual records behind it, which is how published research can be verified and not merely trusted. This applies throughout the site, not just to demographic information.

For example, when a chart shows the number of election candidates whose names have been assigned a particular demographic classification, readers can examine the individual names and classifications that produced that figure. This allows them to challenge our methods, identify mistakes, check the underlying political records and reach their own conclusions. Publishing only aggregate figures would not allow the same level of independent scrutiny.

The legal basis for our processing of ordinary personal data is our legitimate interests, and the public's, in independent research, democratic transparency and the communication and verification of political information (UK GDPR Article 6(1)(f)).

Some of the information we process, including political opinions, religious beliefs and sexual orientation, and estimates relating to these characteristics, is special category data. We rely on the provisions for scientific and historical research in the public interest (Article 9(2)(j) and the Data Protection Act 2018, Schedule 1, paragraph 4) where the statutory conditions and safeguards are met. Where a person has deliberately made the relevant information public themselves, we may also rely on Article 9(2)(e).

We also process personal data for the purpose of publishing journalistic and academic material. We believe there is a substantial public interest both in research into British politics and in enabling readers to scrutinise, down to the level of individual datapoints where necessary, the evidence underlying that research.

Where we reasonably believe that publication is in the public interest and that applying a particular provision of data protection law would be incompatible with these academic or journalistic purposes, we rely, to that extent, on the exemption in the Data Protection Act 2018 (Schedule 2, paragraph 26). In particular, preventing the publication of certain identifiable research observations would impair users' ability to verify or challenge the evidence underlying our analysis or the analysis of other users. The presence of a robust means of investigating, challenging and rigorously scrutinising others' analysis raises the credibility of analysis that has successfully survived this scrutiny.

In deciding what information to publish, we consider its contribution to these purposes, its reliability, the privacy interests of the people concerned and the potential consequences of publication. We distinguish estimates from established facts and provide a means for individuals to challenge information about them.

We do not use this information to make decisions about anyone, to target anyone, or to contact anyone. We have not written to each person individually because that would take disproportionate effort across many thousands of records, most of them historical; this notice is how we tell you.

**People who have died** are not covered by data protection law, and most of the people in the data, which goes back to 1832, are no longer living.

**If something about you is wrong, or you object to it.** Tell us from the [Your data page](/privacy/request). You can ask us to correct a fact, to challenge a guess about you, or to stop showing something. You do not need an account. A person reads every request and answers within one month. Where a fact comes from an official source that is itself wrong (for example the Electoral Commission's register), we will correct our copy where we can verify the error and tell you who holds the source record. A correction we make is recorded, stays in place when the data is rebuilt, and is shown as a correction where the value's source is given.

## 5. Who receives personal data

We do not sell personal data. Apart from what is published on the site itself, it goes only to:

- **Resend** (Plus Five Five, Inc., United States), which sends our emails and so receives your email address and the emails' contents. It acts only on our instructions, under its data processing terms, and keeps a copy of each email and its delivery record for 30 days. Its own service providers include one that checks a sample of outgoing emails for spam and phishing.
- **OpenAI** (United States): its moderation service receives comments as section 2 describes, under its data processing terms for business services; and ChatGPT may be used for the AI-assisted review of public content, also as section 2 describes. OpenAI's language models were also used to prepare parts of the published data from public sources (section 4, and summaries of entries in the Register of Interests).
- **Hosting.** The site runs on a server we rent from IONOS Cloud Ltd., in a data centre in the United Kingdom. Everything the site holds is stored there, including the accounts and what you post. IONOS provides the machine and acts only on our instructions, under its data processing agreement. Visitors reach the site through Cloudflare, Inc. (United States), which protects it from attacks and carries every request, and so sees your internet address and what you send. Cloudflare also acts only on our instructions, under its data processing terms, and does not hold the site's database.
- **The police, the National Crime Agency, a court or a regulator**, where the law requires it or it is necessary to prevent or detect crime.

**Outside the United Kingdom.** IONOS's agreement keeps what it holds for us in the United Kingdom or the European Economic Area, which UK law treats as giving adequate protection; if it ever needed to send any of it further, it must meet the law's requirements for doing so. Resend, OpenAI and Cloudflare are based in the United States. Resend is certified under the UK Extension to the EU-US Data Privacy Framework (the "UK-US data bridge"), which UK law recognises as giving adequate protection to personal data sent to it; its data processing terms also include the UK's standard contractual safeguards. Where OpenAI or Cloudflare acts on our behalf, the transfer is covered by that company's data processing terms, which include the safeguards UK law requires for international transfers. Anything published on the site can of course be read from anywhere in the world.

## 6. How long we keep it

We keep personal data only as long as we need it for the purpose it was collected for. The periods are:

- **Your account (email address, password hash, screen name, settings).** Until you close the account or we remove it. Needed to run the account.
- **What you post: comments, shared charts, scores, new columns, likes and dislikes.** Until you delete them or the account is closed. They are the service.
- **The words of a comment you deleted.** 90 days after you delete it, then erased (its empty place in the thread stays). A report may arrive after a deletion; the law on reporting child sexual abuse material.
- **A copy of a chart, score or new column you deleted, and of an account that was closed or removed.** 90 days, then erased. As above.
- **Reports, with the email address a logged-out reporter chose to leave.** Three years after the report is decided. To answer appeals and complaints, to see patterns of abuse, and to show how we met our online safety duties.
- **Appeals, complaints and messages to the moderator.** Three years after they are closed. As above.
- **Data protection requests and our answers.** Three years after they are closed. To show that requests were answered, and how.
- **The internet address a report, appeal, complaint or data protection request was sent from.** 90 days. To stop the forms being flooded.
- **The moderator's decision log (what was decided about which item or account, and why).** Three years; longer only while the decision is the reason for a suspension or a sign-up bar still in force. To answer appeals, apply the rules consistently and show how we met our online safety duties.
- **Automated flags on a comment (the word list and OpenAI's moderation check).** Until the comment is erased; a flag a moderator has closed, three years at most. So a person reads flagged comments first, and to check how accurate the tools are.
- **The AI-assisted review's assessments of comments and other public content.** Three years. To check the assistant's accuracy and to explain a decision it contributed to.
- **The record of which comments were given to the AI assistant through a temporary key, and when.** Three years. So we can say who your data was given to.
- **Which account or internet address used which score, new column or shared chart.** 90 days; after that only daily totals, which name nobody. To decide what to keep ready in memory, to limit heavy use, and to show how often something is used.
- **A hashed form of the internet addresses an account logs in and posts from.** 90 days after the address was last used. To stop a banned person opening a new account.
- **The bar on signing up again after an account is removed or suspended for a serious breach, or barred by a moderator in another serious case.** The hashed email address: no end date. Hashed internet addresses: 180 days. An ended bar: 90 days more. To keep a person banned for child sexual abuse, terrorism or repeated serious breaches off the service.
- **A sign-up waiting for its email link; a password-reset link.** 24 hours; 1 hour. Needed only until the link is used.

Deletions are carried out automatically each time the site's server starts, and we restart it at least every 14 days, so an item can be kept for up to 14 days beyond the period given above, never longer.

Information about people in public political life (section 4) is kept as a historical and research record, corrected where it is shown to be wrong.

## 7. Your rights

You have the right to:

- **see** the personal data we hold about you and have a copy of it;
- have it **corrected** if it is wrong or incomplete;
- have it **erased**, including by closing your account;
- **object** to our using it, or ask us to **limit** how we use it;
- receive what you gave us in a form you can **take elsewhere**;
- **complain** about how we have handled it.

Use the [Your data page](/privacy/request) for any of these. It works with or without an account. If you are logged in you can also download what your account has given us and posted, and close your account, straight away from that page. We acknowledge every request, a person reads it, and we answer within one month (the law allows up to two more months for a complex request, and we will tell you if we need them). It is free. We may need to check that you are who you say you are before we send anything.

**What an answer includes.** If you ask for your data you get what you ask for, including records only the moderator normally sees: automated flags on your comments, the AI assistant's assessments of them, the record of how your comments have fared, and the decisions made about your content or account. We leave out only what the law lets us withhold: other people's data (who reported you, and which accounts have blocked you; we do tell you how many have) and, where it applies, information whose disclosure would get in the way of preventing or detecting crime.

**Limits.** These rights are not absolute. For example, we keep some records after you close an account for the periods in section 6, because the law or other people's safety requires it.

Data protection law also provides certain exemptions for scientific research, journalism and academic publication. We apply these only where the relevant statutory conditions are met, rather than as a general exemption from your rights.

If you ask us to correct, remove or restrict the use of information about you, we will consider your request individually, including the potential impact of publication on you, the public interest in the information and the effect that complying with your request would have on our research or journalistic purposes. We will explain our decision and any exemption on which we rely.

**When you close your account** your comments, shared charts, likes and dislikes are taken off the site, together with other people's replies to your comments. A copy is kept out of public view for 90 days and then erased. A score or new column of yours that someone else's work builds on is kept, hidden and with no owner, so that their work keeps working.

## 8. Complaints

If you are unhappy with how we have used your personal data, please tell us first, from the [Your data page](/privacy/request) (choose the complaint option). We will acknowledge your complaint within 30 days, look into it and tell you the outcome.

You also have the right to complain to the Information Commissioner's Office, the UK's data protection regulator: ico.org.uk, or 0303 123 1113.

## 9. Changes to this notice

We will change this notice when what we do with personal data changes, and before the change takes effect where we can. The date at the top will change, and a change that matters will be announced on the site.
